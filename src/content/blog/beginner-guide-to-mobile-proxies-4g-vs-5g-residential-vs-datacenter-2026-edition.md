## Understanding 4G, 5G and Proxy Types  

When you choose a mobile proxy, the underlying network technology (4G LTE or 5G NR) and the source of the IP address (residential mobile vs datacenter) determine speed, reliability and how likely you are to trigger anti‑fraud systems.  

**4G vs 5G in Malaysia**  
Maxis, CelcomDigi and Digi have rolled out 5G in Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Johor Bahru and the Klang Valley, offering peak speeds of 1‑2 Gbps in ideal conditions. 4G LTE still provides solid 20‑50 Mbps downstream, which is more than enough for most web‑scraping tasks. The practical difference for proxy users is latency: 5G can cut round‑trip times from ~40 ms to ~15 ms, making real‑time interactions (like bidding on Lazada flash sales) smoother. However, 5G coverage is still patchy outside major cities, so a 4G‑based proxy from a reliable Malaysian carrier guarantees connectivity in Penang’s industrial zones or Kuala Lumpur’s suburban areas where 5G may drop to 4G fallback.

**Residential mobile vs datacenter IPs**  
A residential mobile proxy routes your request through an actual SIM card attached to a smartphone or modem on Maxis, CelcomDigi or Digi’s network. Websites see a genuine mobile user agent and IP, which dramatically reduces CAPTCHAs and bans on Shopee, Lazada and Mudah.my. Datacenter proxies, by contrast, originate from servers in data centres; they are faster and cheaper but are easily flagged as non‑human traffic, especially on Malaysian e‑commerce platforms that aggressively block known datacenter ranges.

For beginners, the safest bet is a 4G residential mobile proxy from a local carrier. If you need ultra‑low latency for high‑frequency automation (e.g., monitoring price changes on Lazada every minute), upgrading to a 5G residential proxy gives you the edge without sacrificing the trust factor of a real mobile IP.
